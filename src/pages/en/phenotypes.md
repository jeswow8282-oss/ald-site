**ALD looks like one disease, but it presents as several.**

There is a single cause — the *ABCD1* gene on the X chromosome. The consequences are not single. Brothers carrying the identical variant in the same family can follow entirely different courses.

So **"it is ALD" settles very little about what comes next.** Which form, and at what point in it, have to be read together.

This page gathers those branches on one page. The detail sits on the individual pages.

---

## Four branches

### Cerebral ALD

- **Who** — mainly boys; adult men develop it too
- **When** — childhood peak at **4 to 10**; in adults, **the thirties**
- **How** — inflammatory demyelination of the brain, progressing **over months to a few years**

→ [Cerebral ALD](/en/cerebral-ald)

### Adrenomyeloneuropathy (AMN)

- **Who** — nearly all men; women develop the same form
- **When** — mean age **27** in men, **41** in women
- **How** — slow damage to the spinal cord **over years to decades**

→ [Adrenomyeloneuropathy](/en/amn)

### Women with ALD

- **Who** — women carrying an *ABCD1* variant
- **When** — usually **after middle age**
- **How** — the same spinal cord symptoms as male AMN, progressing more slowly

→ [Women with ALD](/en/women)

### Adrenal insufficiency

- **Who** — **over 80%** of males
- **When** — **half of them before age 10**
- **How** — hormone production falls. **Infection or stress can make it dangerous abruptly**

→ [Adrenal insufficiency](/en/adrenal-insufficiency)

In women, **cerebral disease essentially does not occur, and adrenal insufficiency is uncommon.** Girls and women are therefore not subject to routine cerebral surveillance.

## Where timing decides, and where it does not

What "urgent" means differs by branch, and the difference matters.

### Where timing changes the outcome

**Cerebral ALD** — the one form of ALD in which timing determines the result. Found early, before symptoms, it can be halted; found late, there is no way back. This is why periodic brain MRI is necessary.

**Adrenal insufficiency** — it develops gradually, but an intercurrent infection or stress can precipitate an **adrenal crisis** that becomes life-threatening within hours. It is a danger that preparation removes.

### Where the horizon is long

**AMN** and **women with ALD** — these do not deteriorate abruptly. What they require instead is management sustained over decades. Men with AMN remain at risk of converting to cerebral disease, so surveillance continues.

---

## The branches are not mutually exclusive

A person does not belong to only one of them.

- Adrenal insufficiency frequently arrives **before any neurological symptom.** Sometimes it is the first clue.
- An adult man with AMN may **convert to cerebral disease.**
- Someone treated for cerebral disease in childhood may still **develop AMN as an adult.** Transplantation does not affect myeloneuropathy.

"We have AMN, so cerebral disease is not our concern" does not hold.

---

## Two things that are commonly got wrong

**"Women are carriers"**

They are not. A woman carrying an *ABCD1* variant is a patient. More than 80% develop spinal cord symptoms in adult life. The 2022 international consensus recommendations classify them as patients rather than carriers.
→ [Women with ALD](/en/women)

**"The genetic result tells you which form it will be"**

It does not. No consistent relationship between variant type and phenotype has been established, and identical twins have been reported to follow different courses. Plasma fatty acid levels do not predict it either.
→ [Why it happens, and why it differs between people](/en/pathogenesis)
